# Ruins sketchbook

52 archaeological studies cover every current entry in the landmark list’s **Ruins** group (abandoned cities with no forgotten phase, tier 1–2). Each of the 50 sites has English and Chinese titles and captions, sources, and dedicated artwork. Pueblo Bonito and Chichén Itzá each have two views.

## Art direction

Use the existing field-sketchbook style: visible graphite, sepia hatching, restrained watercolour, unfinished cream-paper margins. Alternate streets, interiors, architectural details, earthworks and landscape views. People are optional. Punic Carthage and Roman Carthago deliberately have distinct subjects. Do not reconstruct missing buildings, add generic explorer portraits, or turn modest archaeological remains into spectacular invented monuments.

These are interpretive studies of surviving archaeological fabric, including excavation and conservation. They are not snapshots of the abandonment year, and abandonment is not a claim that all habitation or religious activity permanently ceased. The existing dataset dates are retained. The ruin era begins at that date; landmark-list opening moves to the later of the viewpoint’s known date and the abandonment date, while list ordering remains unchanged. Scrubbing back before abandonment hides this era.

## Assets and reproducibility

Built-in imagegen, one request per asset. Accepted images: `data/depictions/city/<id>/ruins.webp`. Full generation prompts and method: adjacent `ruins.json`. Editorial metadata, subject-specific constraints and source evidence: `subject.json`. Chinese: `data/i18n/zh/depictions.json`, keys `city/<id>.ruins`. Sources: `ruin-study-<id>` in `data/sources.csv`. The normal data build creates card, thumbnail and full-size variants.

## Companion views within an era

Pueblo Bonito (`chaco`) adds **The great house from the rim**, showing the D-shaped room blocks, plazas and circular kivas in their canyon setting. Chichén Itzá adds **El Castillo across the plaza**, a wide view of the nine-tiered pyramid and summit temple. Both retain their original close study as the first page.

Each additional entry has a unique ID (`ruins-panorama`) and `companionOf: "ruins"`, with exactly the same `from`/`to` dates as its main era. The data builder validates the reference and dates; only main eras participate in the non-overlap check. Companion references cannot form chains. Full prompts and references are saved in each `ruins-panorama.json`; accepted art is `ruins-panorama.webp` in the same city folder, generated with built-in imagegen.

City cards expose the existing sketch pager and named thumbnails for companion views. Changing views within the active era preserves the year; moving out of that era resets the view. Other city-era navigation and journey pages keep their existing behavior.
